# The steady step's operator from the second order on

Design for #395, written before any code. The steady implicit step solves (V/Δt + J̃) ΔU = −R(U), J̃ the approximate Jacobian assembled in 5 × 5 blocks ([euler.md](euler.md#time-stepping)): the faces' dF/dW with the reconstruction's gradients and limiter held fixed. It is solved by GCR preconditioned by one multigrid cycle on the same matrix ([multigrid.md](multigrid.md)), or by the line sweeps (`"lines"`), to a relative residual of 0.1 in at most 10 iterations.

## Problem, measured

On the TMR flat plate 69×49 (#395; `physics/ns-sa` at c3966068, SA and laminar), once the SER raises the CFL number past ~10³ at second order, the iteration settles on a period-2 cycle: every step is taken in full (α = 1), its linear solve meets the tolerance, and the state flips between two states for thousands of steps. Before #277 the plate converged only because a round-off residual pinned the CFL number at 1. At a state of the cycle, central differences of the residual give ‖(J − J̃) v‖ / ‖J v‖ of 10⁻⁹–10⁻² at first order, 0.15–0.25 at second order away from the wall, and 0.38–0.70 on the density and energy rows of the wall cells (aspect ratio 10³–10⁴). The limiter (off: the same cycle), the SA coupling (laminar: the same stall), the boundary fit (off: no better, #395) and the linear solve (it meets its tolerance) are ruled out. The missing part is the convective reconstruction's dependence on the neighbours' states through the frozen gradients: a defect correction with an operator that far from J overshoots by a factor 2 once V/Δt no longer dominates.

## Change

**From the step after the switch to second order, the linear solve's operator is the true Jacobian, applied matrix-free; J̃ stays the preconditioner.** GCR solves (V/Δt + J) ΔU = −R(U), the product being

  (V/Δt) v + [R(U + ε v) − R(U)] / ε,

R the residual the step reports (second order, the boundary conditions, `ν̃` held: see SA below) with its non-differentiable switches frozen at U: the limiter's φ and the reconstruction's positivity fallback (`reconstruct` falling back to the cell value) take the values they have at U, so the product is J with the limiter frozen, one linear operator for the whole solve. Without it, a perturbation of ~10⁻⁴ (`fp32+acc64`) crosses the limiter's min/max switches near shocks and the operator changes between Krylov iterations. The reconstruction's gradients are not frozen: they are what J̃ misses. The products are evaluated in a workspace of their own (state, primitives, gradients, φ copied from U, residual): the step's `state_`, `res_` and the evaluator's cache at U, which the history, the norms, the round-off floor and the SA step read, are never overwritten; preconditioned exactly as now: one multigrid cycle on V/Δt + J̃ (`"multigrid"`), or the line sweeps on it (`"lines"`). Tolerance (0.1), iteration limit (10), the failure rule (0.9: step rejected) and the SER are unchanged. This is the inexact Newton-Krylov step with an approximate-Jacobian preconditioner of FUN3D's HANIM (Wang, Diskin, Nielsen and Liu 2021, AIAA SciTech) and of ADflow's ANK at its matrix-free level (Yildirim, Kenway, Mader and Martins 2019, J. Comput. Phys. 397:108741), kept inside main's SER rather than their CFL controls (#207 measured those slower here).

**Activation**: the step after the one that switches to second order (`switch_order`, logged as the `solver` info event of #356, or the first step of a run continued past the switch), on the host's steady step only, with `"multigrid"` or `"lines"`. Unsteady runs (explicit SSP-RK3, no linear solve) are unchanged. The first-order phase keeps the assembled operator: there J̃ is the Jacobian up to the viscous blocks (measured above: 10⁻⁹ away from the wall), so a residual per Krylov iteration would buy nothing. `"gauss-seidel"` (fixed sweeps, no Krylov method) is unchanged, and so is the device: its steady step is explicit, with no linear solve ([euler.md](euler.md)). The scope is the host's steady path. A `linear` info event marks the first matrix-free step. The state that decides it (`second_order_`) is already in the restart record, so a continued run repeats the uninterrupted one bit for bit.

**ε** (Knoll and Keyes 2004, J. Comput. Phys. 193:357–397, §2.2, the form scaled by the state), on scaled unknowns: each equation's unknowns are divided by its freestream scale s_k (ρ∞, ρ∞ q∞ for each momentum, ρ∞ E∞, q∞ = |v∞| + c∞), so that density, momentum and energy weigh alike; ε = √ε_R (1 + ‖S⁻¹U‖) / ‖S⁻¹v‖, ‖·‖ the root mean square over the owned unknowns (exact sums over the ranks, so every rank uses the same ε), U the conserved state (not the stored difference from the reference), ε_R the machine epsilon of the precision the state is stored in: 2⁻⁵² in `fp64`, 2⁻²³ in `fp32+acc64`. An unknown whose scaled |v_i| and |U_i| are of the rms sizes is then perturbed by about √ε_R |U_i| (√ε_R ≈ 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ in `fp32+acc64`, 2⁹ times the FP32 unit in the last place of the stored difference, at most |U_i| + |U_ref|); a perturbation built from the FP64 epsilon (10⁻⁸ relative) would round away there. A scalar ε cannot guarantee both bounds for every unknown, so each product checks the perturbed state before evaluating it:

- admissible and small: every cell's density and pressure at U + εv stay within 10 % of their values at U; otherwise ε is halved, at most ten times;
- representable: the stored state actually receives the perturbation, ‖S⁻¹(δ − εv)‖ ≤ 10⁻² ‖S⁻¹ εv‖ with δ = stored(U + εv) − stored(U).

The halving decision is a maximum over the ranks, so every rank uses the same ε. A zero direction (‖S⁻¹v‖ = 0) has the zero product, without an evaluation. A product that meets neither within ten halvings fails the step's solve (a linear failure: the step is rejected, the next CFL number halves); the step's largest halving count and representability ratio are in the `linear` warning's data. With the perturbation bounded to 10 % in density and pressure and of order √ε_R relative where v is of its rms size, the one-sided difference's truncation error is of the order of the relative perturbation, and the residual's rounding in `fp32+acc64` (FP32 compute, ≤ ε₃₂ Φ_k per equation) enters as ε₃₂ / √ε₃₂ = √ε₃₂ relative; the agreement test below bounds both where J̃ is exact. The difference R(U + εv) − R(U) is taken in FP64 from the two residual arrays (FP64 in both precisions: `fp32_acc64::residual` is `double`). No false convergence: the history and the convergence test keep using R(U), not the linear residual; the round-off floor rule of `fp32+acc64` is unchanged.

**SA**: the mean-flow product holds ν̃, and so μ_t, at the current state; the SA equation keeps its loosely coupled step after the flow's. What the SA step reads from the flow (the primitives `SpalartAllmaras::eddy_viscosity` and the SA residual take) stays at U, since the products use their own workspace. The SA rows are not in the matrix-free operator: the coupling cost (ν̃ lagging the flow) is what the laminar/SA comparison of the gate measures. Coupling ν̃ into the product (6 equations, μ_t from the perturbed ν̃ before the flow residual) is #235's scope.

**Cost**: one residual evaluation per GCR iteration (at most 10 per step) in place of a block matrix product, plus, per product, a pass over the cells for the admissibility and representability checks and two collective reductions (the halving decision and the norms); the preconditioner, the assembly and the hierarchy are unchanged. All of it counts in the cost gate. Memory: the products' workspace, a second copy of the evaluator's per-cell arrays (state, primitives, gradients, φ, residual).

## Gate, declared before any run

Flat plate (`physics/tmr/case.sh flatplate`, fp64, 6000 steps), with this change merged into `physics/ns-sa`; measure: the first step at which every equation but the identically zero z-momentum is ten orders below its peak (#395). Not the runtime's rule: it counts orders from iteration 1, where the plate's uniform start leaves density at 4.6 × 10⁻⁸ against 6.0 for ρu, so ten orders of density would ask for 10⁻¹⁷–10⁻¹⁸, below its round-off; the peak is #395's measure, used for every commit there. The same run must also end with every such equation at most 10⁻¹⁰ of its peak at step 6000 (no transient dip counted).

- laminar 69×49, then SA 69×49: at most 3332 steps (2777 × 1.2);
- SA 35×25: at most 2260 steps (1883 × 1.2, #207);
- SA 137×97: within the 6000 steps (#207: not reached in 3000 at CFL 1).

No regression, against `main` at the merge base, steps to ten orders (fp64, the runtime's convergence):

- Euler MMS hex/tet/poly (16–48), Ni's bump 96/384, NACA 0012 (M 0.5 α 1.25°, M 0.8 α 0°, M 0.8 α 1.25°) at 128² and 256²: within 5 % or fewer steps; forces at convergence within 10⁻⁸ |c| + 10⁻¹⁰.
- Cost: CPU time to convergence of autan-run on the same cases and the plates, reported against `main` with the time per step; on the Euler cases at most 1.30 × `main`'s (the residual per Krylov iteration is the price; above, the gate fails, the activation is not retuned on the results).
- `"lines"` under `reproducibility = "ranks"`: NACA M 0.5 α 1.25° 64² and M 0.8 α 0° 64² on 1, 2 and 4 ranks, `history.jsonl` byte-identical (#282's gate).
- `fp32+acc64`: the laminar plate 69×49 and NACA M 0.8 α 0° 256² end at their round-off floor (status as `main`'s, the NACA's last step within the floor of [euler.md](euler.md#precision)), the NACA's surface Cp within 0.001 of its `fp64` run outside the shocks and its CL, CD and CM within 0.1 % of the `fp64` run's ([acceptance.md](../validation/acceptance.md)); the flat plate's skin friction at x = 0.97 within 1 % of its `fp64` run.
- Accuracy (AGENTS.md): Euler MMS on hex, tet, poly and hybrid: `fp64` observed orders, `euler-mms-order` PASS, unchanged from `main`'s; `fp32+acc64` agreeing with `fp64` level by level (`autan-vv agree`, [acceptance.md](../validation/acceptance.md)).

Tests: the matrix-free product agrees with the assembled one where J̃ is the Jacobian (a first-order state of the verification case: within 10 √ε_R of its magnitude, in both precisions); a direction whose perturbation rounds away in the stored state fails the solve; a run logs the first matrix-free step at the step after the switch to second order and not before; a run continued across the switch repeats the uninterrupted one bit for bit, and so does a run continued at any step of the matrix-free phase (a product leaking into the reported state or residual would make the continued run's first line differ); near a shock (NACA M 0.8), two products of the same direction in one solve are bit for bit equal (the frozen limiter).

## Ownership

The steady step is CFD - Euler's (`physics/src/euler_physics.cpp`, reviewed by them); the GCR and the multigrid's outer operator are `solvers/` (a `Multigrid::solve` taking the outer product). [multigrid.md](multigrid.md) and [euler.md](euler.md) state the operator and link here.
