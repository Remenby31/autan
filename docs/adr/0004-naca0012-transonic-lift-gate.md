# 0004: The NACA 0012 transonic lift gate: a GCI interval against the four-code range of Vassberg and Jameson

**Status:** proposed, 2026-11-07; item 6 amended 2026-11-08 (runs on `main`, #244 in beta), before any run. **Decided by:** Meta (the lead's delegate for CFD), relayed by the orchestrator. **Issues:** #415, #566, #244, #183.

## Context

`naca0012-m080-a125-cl` (M 0.8, α 1.25°, point-vortex far field) passes when our continuum CL lies inside 0.3516–0.3562, widened by our GCI (`acceptance.md`). The band is the range of the two point-vortex codes in Vassberg and Jameson (2010), Table 11, p. 1165: CFL3D + vortex 0.351596613 and FLO82 0.356208937. Each is an extrapolation of low-order data. FLO82's CL has order 1.151, from 1024², 2048² and 4096² (Table 3, p. 1159). CFL3D + vortex's CL has order 0.289, from 512², 1024² and 2048² (Table 9, p. 1164). The paper gives no uncertainty for either, only the spread across its four codes (Table 11). It finds FLO82's transonic lifting coefficients first order (p. 1159), and states that at transonic conditions the order of accuracy deteriorates for all of the methods (abstract, p. 1152).

Table 11 (p. 1165), M 0.8, α 1.25°, continuum CL: FLO82 0.356208937, OVERFLOW v2.1t 0.351662793, CFL3Dv6 0.348226045, CFL3Dv6 + vortex 0.351596613; Min 0.348226045, Max 0.356208937, spread 0.007983097.

Our geometry and grids are the paper's: the sharp-trailing-edge NACA 0012 (eqs. 1, 4) and the Karman–Trefftz O-grids nested from 4096² (eqs. 9–16), in `autan_vv.meshes.naca0012`. A level is NC = 4096 / 2^k. 4096² (16.8 M cells) is out of reach of a DGX Spark, so 2048² is the finest level we run.

## Decision

1. **Continuum.** CL∞ comes from the three finest levels of the family (ratio 2), by the procedure of Celik et al. (Steps 3–5, eqs. 3a–3c, 4 and 7, pp. 4–5). It is implemented in `autan_vv.order.richardson`. For f1, f2, f3 on the fine, medium and coarse levels, with ε21 = f2 − f1 and ε32 = f3 − f2:
   - **monotone triplet required**: ε21 and ε32 nonzero and of the same sign;
   - observed order p_obs = ln(ε32/ε21) / ln 2 (eq. 3a with r = 2);
   - extrapolation with **p_eff = min(p_obs, 2)**, the formal order of the scheme, so that the extrapolation never assumes a higher order than the scheme has (ours);
   - CL∞ = f1 − ε21 / (2^p_eff − 1) (eq. 4);
   - absolute uncertainty U = Fs |ε21| / (2^p_eff − 1), the fine-grid GCI of eq. 7 times |f1|;
   - the interval is [CL∞ − U, CL∞ + U].

   Celik et al. give U as the uncertainty of the fine-level value f1. Centring it on CL∞ and capping the order at 2 are policy choices (Meta's decision), not their prescription, and nothing in their procedure justifies the recentring. We compare the extrapolated value, not the fine-level one, with the published continuum values, and keep the half-width Celik et al. attach to the fine level. For a fixed Fs ≥ 1, the cap moves both ends of the interval outward compared with the uncapped one.

   A triplet with no continuum gets no verdict but its code: not monotone (V0107), two equal values (V0106), no positive p (V0108, so p ≤ 0 or undefined), or p > 64 (V0109). No least-squares or other fit replaces the three-level estimate. Celik et al. state that the procedure does not work when a difference is close to zero or the convergence oscillates, and ask for additional grid refinement (pp. 4–5). Rule 4 then applies.
2. **Safety factor (Meta's decision).** Fs = 1.25, the factor of eq. 7 (p. 5), when the three finest levels are monotone and 0.5 ≤ p_obs ≤ 2.5. Otherwise Fs = 3.0; Celik et al. give no value for that case, so 3.0 is ours.

   Why a range and not p_obs ≈ 2: the scheme is second order in smooth regions, but a captured shock limits integrated forces to first order. Vassberg and Jameson find FLO82's transonic lifting coefficients first order (1.151, 1.118 and 1.148, Table 3, p. 1159), CFL3D's lifting drag near first order (0.967, Table 8, and 0.960 with the vortex, Table 9, p. 1164), and the order deteriorating for every code at transonic conditions (abstract, p. 1152). An observed order between first and second order is therefore the expected behaviour here, not a sign of a pre-asymptotic family. Celik et al. note that agreement of the observed order with the formal order indicates the asymptotic range, and that disagreement should not by itself be taken as a sign of unsatisfactory calculations (p. 4).
3. **Pass.** The gate passes when U ≤ 0.003991446, half the width of the four-code range (0.356208937 − 0.348226045 = 0.007982892; Table 11 prints a spread of 0.007983097, and we take the endpoints), and [CL∞ − U, CL∞ + U] overlaps the four-code range of Table 11 (p. 1165), 0.348226045–0.356208937, that is, when CL∞ − U ≤ 0.356208937 and CL∞ + U ≥ 0.348226045 (bounds inclusive). A continuum whose interval does not overlap the range fails. A continuum with U above the spread fails as not informative: as p_obs → 0⁺ with Fs = 3.0, U grows without bound and would overlap any finite range, so the overlap alone would show compatibility, not accuracy. U is a half-width; with the ceiling, our whole interval is never wider than the four-code range. A PASS therefore shows that our continuum is compatible with the four codes with an ESTIMATED discretisation uncertainty no larger than their disagreement. It does not measure the true error.
4. **No continuum.** The four levels 256², 512², 1024² and 2048² are all run; the verdict uses 512², 1024² and 2048² only. 2048² is the finest level a DGX Spark runs (4096² is out of reach), and our generator has no other family (NC = 4096 / 2^k), so no level can be added. If that triplet gives no continuum (rule 1), the gate fails. No tolerance, band, factor, level or family is chosen after the runs.
5. **Diagnostic, not a criterion.** The report gives, at every level common to Table 3, the gap Autan − FLO82, beside the per-level gaps of the other codes above.
6. **Sequence.** *(Amended 2026-11-08, before any run: #244 moved to beta by Meta's decision on 08/11, so the alpha gate no longer waits for it.)* This applies to runs made on a commit of `main` named in the manifest below, with #244 not merged: levels 256², 512², 1024² and 2048², fp64, point vortex. A later verdict on a commit that merges #244 is a beta matter and follows the same rules with a new manifest. Before the first run, a manifest is posted on #415 and not changed afterwards. It gives that commit, this ADR's merged revision, every case and mesh hash, the stopping rule, the force-extraction rule and the retry policy (a run is retried only after a machine failure, never after a result). Every run is kept, including failed ones. The H1 study of #415 resumes within this frame. Until then, the gate's status is the one recorded on #566.

## Consequences

- The `naca0012-m080-a125-cl` row changes in `acceptance.md`; the CD part of the row does not.
- The implementation is in the same PR (#589), with every consumer:
  - `autan_vv.cases.naca0012`: the gate bounds, Fs per rule 2, and the overlap test;
  - `autan_vv.order.richardson`: Fs and the order cap as parameters, the cap applied to both CL∞ and U, and only for this gate (the other gates keep their extrapolation); its guards (it rejects p > 64 and 0 < p ≤ 10⁻⁸) are reconciled with rule 1's codes, so that the code and this ADR give the same verdict on every triplet;
  - `vv/tests/test_naca0012.py`;
  - `docs/validation/cases.md`;
  - datasets §M2.
- A low observed order widens U: at p = 0.3 with Fs = 3.0, U ≈ 13 |ε21|. The ceiling of rule 3 bounds this: such a triplet passes only if 13 |ε21| ≤ 0.003991446. The per-level gap of rule 5 is reported beside the verdict.
- The CD gates and the M 0.5 gates are unchanged.
